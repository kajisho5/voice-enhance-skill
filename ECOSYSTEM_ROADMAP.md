## Wiring

ffmpeg-skill `probe` (input facts, stem validation) and `audio` (stream extraction, mono fold-down) are used by ISOLATE ([#4](https://github.com/kajisho5/voice-enhance-skill/pull/4)). DEREVERB ([#5](https://github.com/kajisho5/voice-enhance-skill/pull/5)) uses `probe` and a single `audio --audio-stream` pass (WPE takes any rate and layout, ADR-004). BLEED_REDUCE ([#6](https://github.com/kajisho5/voice-enhance-skill/pull/6)) prepares every track like ISOLATE at the first track's rate (ADR-005). `voice-enhance remux` ([#7](https://github.com/kajisho5/voice-enhance-skill/pull/7)) puts a stem back with one `audio --replace` call and checks the copied video stream through `probe` (ADR-006). Gaps to report to ffmpeg-skill: `audio --sample-rate` (inputs not at 48 kHz are refused until it exists), `audio --mono` reading the first audio stream's channel count instead of the one `--audio-stream` picks (ADR-003), `.wav` output fixed at `pcm_s16le`, so 24-bit sources reach the engine at 16 bits (ADR-004, not blocking), and no way to pick one channel of a stream (`audio --channel N`), so two microphones sharing a stereo stream are folded into one BLEED_REDUCE track (ADR-005). BLEED_REDUCE with tracks at different rates also waits for `audio --sample-rate`. For remux (ADR-006): `audio --replace` keeps only the first video stream and drops every other audio stream, so a file with two video streams is refused and one with several audio streams needs `--drop-other-audio`; there is no packet-hash tool, so byte identity of the video is asserted in the tests, not by the op.
